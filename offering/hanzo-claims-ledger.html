The Hanzo Claims Ledger

Every claim you could make, sorted by whether it survives a skeptical engineer with a terminal. Market the top tier. Source the middle. Retire the bottom.

Why grade claims at all

Your buyer is a technical founder who will check. One claim that collapses under scrutiny costs more credibility than three verified ones earn — and it retroactively taints the true ones. The discipline below is the asset: a page where every number is checkable is itself the proof that the engineering is honest.

Tier 1 — Proven

Say these everywhere

Measured directly. A stranger can reproduce each in under a minute.

We took gRPC to zero.

65 gRPC packages → 0. Two roots: the Gemini SDK (sole importer of cloud.google.com/go/auth, which dragged gax-go and all 13 s2a-go packages) and an OTLP exporter whose HTTP variant still imported gRPC internally. Replaced with a ~190-line native REST client and a ZAP-native span exporter.

go list -deps ./cmd/cloud | grep -c google.golang.org/grpc

One service binary went from 3,253 packages to 652.

Every "standalone" service binary was linking the entire composition root. Fixing the generator cut 250,481 → 63,522 total package-links across 77 binaries — mean 3,253 → 825, floor 652.

go list -deps ./cmd/wallets | wc -l

Our wire protocol decodes with zero allocations.

Headers are length-prefixed name/value pairs; every name and value is a subslice of the frame. 82ns and 1 alloc/op → 26ns and 0 allocs/op — 3.2× — replacing a JSON map inside the frame.

Published benchmark in the zip repo.

A plugin can panic and the host keeps serving.

Lazy start on first request, hot reload with no dropped requests, and a failed build that cannot take a route down — each pinned by a test passing under -race, not asserted in a README.

go test -race -run 'TestPlugins_SurvivesPanic|TestLoad_Lazy'

One registry. Five projections.

A typed handler produces the REST route, the OpenAPI path, the MCP tool, the CLI command, and the SDK client — from one definition. Doc comments are lifted into the spec at build time, so the reference cannot drift from the code.

Hit /.well-known/openapi.json and /mcp on any service.

Tier 3 — Retire

These lose you more than they win

4,000 projects. — retracted twice; the real figure is higher

Measured with the authenticated API as the account that owns the estate: 186 organisations, 2,253 public + 2,172 private org repositories = 4,425, plus 548 personal. 4,973 total. Largest: hanzoskill 749 · hanzoai 591 · luxfi 407 · nko3 388 · Dream-AI-4444 336. Still excludes the self-hosted forge at git.hanzo.ai.

This ledger got it wrong three times — first from one personal account (548), then from a guessed list of twelve orgs (973). Both used unauthenticated endpoints, which return only PUBLIC org membership. The lesson generalises: measure with the credential that can see the whole estate, or do not publish the number.

gh api user/orgs --paginate --jq '.[].login' then per org gh api orgs/<o> --jq '[.public_repos, .total_private_repos]'

One engineer runs all of it.

4,973 repositories, 186 organisations, ~104 services in a single binary, across inference, agents, identity, KMS, billing, commerce, observability, consensus, post-quantum cryptography, FHE and GPU kernels — solo. Set against OpenStack's thousands of contributors or GitLab's 2,000-person company, this is the ratio that has no peer and no counterargument.

It is also the answer to the question every investor asks next — how is this maintainable by one person? — and the answer is the architecture itself: one wire protocol, one registry with five projections, one plugin model. The discipline is not a stylistic preference; it is the only reason the surface is survivable at this headcount.

State the headcount plainly. It is the most load-bearing number here.

Every AI modality ever.

Invites "what about protein folding, weather, robotics, EEG?" — a free win for a skeptic. The accurate version is stronger anyway: text, vision, audio, video, 3D and code under one framework.

Bigger than Clojure / Rich Hickey's life work.

Clojure is deliberately small — that is its thesis. Measuring by volume uses the one metric you both reject, and to an audience that knows him it reads as not understanding the work you cite as an influence.

We dwarf what OpenAI and Anthropic have built.

This is the most expensive sentence in the deck. They run frontier training — nine-figure compute, research organisations. You do not, and an investor knows it in the first second. Say this and everything after it is discounted, including the true claims.

The frame that wins instead: they sell a model behind an API. Nobody gets identity, billing, commerce, compliance, self-hosting or settlement from them — and every company building on them still has to assemble that. You are the layer they will never build, and you run on any model including theirs. Not a competitor. The platform their customers need in order to ship.

The positioning line

The assembled alternative is Supabase + Lago + Keycloak + Grafana + a chain node — five vendors, five auth models, five integration surfaces. Hanzo is one binary, one protocol, one identity. 100+ funded companies already shipped on it.

Breadth is the explanation, never the claim. Lead with who shipped; let the eight domains explain how.

Campaign spine

Five posts, ordered so each earns the audience for the next. Every one leads with a number a reader can reproduce — that is the through-line, and it is also the brand.

  1. "We deleted gRPC from a 3,000-package binary. Here's what was actually pulling it in." Pure engineering, zero pitch. The twist — the HTTP OTLP exporter secretly imports gRPC — is a genuinely useful finding that thousands of Go teams share and none have noticed. This is the post that travels.
  2. "Every 'standalone' binary in our monorepo was linking all 104 services. 3,253 packages → 652." Universal monorepo pain, one-line diagnosis, reproducible command. Ends on the plugin architecture, so the product enters as the answer to a problem the reader now recognizes in their own repo.
  3. "One registry, five projections: REST, OpenAPI, MCP, CLI and SDK from a single handler." The architectural thesis, and the one competitors cannot retrofit. Land the MCP angle hard — every AI company is currently hand-writing tool definitions that drift.
  4. "The eight-domain matrix: what it takes to replace five vendors with one binary." Now the positioning post lands, because posts 1–3 established you measure honestly. Publish the matrix with its caveat intact and invite corrections — the arguments are the distribution.
  5. "100+ funded companies were built on this. Here are three of them." The closer, and the one that converts. It only works after the credibility from 1–4, and it requires the case studies that do not exist yet. Start collecting them now — this is the long-lead item.

Personal brand

The engineering is far ahead of the story. The gap is not talent or output — it is that reach comes from concentration, and your public surface is spread across 548 repositories.

Stop

Comparative superlatives. "Bigger than X" invites scoring, and scoring is where volume loses. Torvalds has 12 public repos and 313k followers; Hickey has 10 and 6.4k. Repo count is inversely correlated with reach — 548 repos split the attention that one would compound.

Start

Be the person who publishes the number. Every post opens with a measurement and the command to reproduce it. That is a rare and defensible identity, it is already how you work, and it makes the claims self-verifying — the reader checks one, trusts the rest.

Own the name

Hattori Hanzō made a shogun. Samurai and Iga ninja commander under Tokugawa Ieyasu. When Nobunaga was killed at Honnō-ji in 1582 and Ieyasu was stranded in hostile territory, Hanzō led him out through Iga — the crossing that kept him alive. Ieyasu went on to found the shogunate that unified Japan for 250 years. There is still a gate at the Imperial Palace named Hanzōmon.

That is the literal king-maker story, and it is far stronger than the swordsmith of the films. Not the man who wins the battle — the one without whom the person who wins it never survives to fight.

Recruiting angle

Lead with scope per engineer. The pitch is not "we're big," it is "you will touch consensus, post-quantum crypto, GPU kernels and inference in the same month, and nobody will slow you down." That is a self-selecting filter for the people you actually want.
