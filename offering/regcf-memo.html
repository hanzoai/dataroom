1 · Thesis

Hanzo AI is an open-source AI cloud and Business OS: one binary supplying the identity, billing, commerce, observability, inference, agents and back-office surface a company otherwise assembles from two hundred vendors. The wager is that the marginal cost of running a company is collapsing toward the cost of running software, and whoever owns the substrate — open, self-hostable, one protocol end to end — becomes the layer the next generation of very small, very large companies is built on. The founder is the existence proof, not a metaphor for one: 4,973 repositories across 186 GitHub organizations, ~104 services in a single Go binary, operated by one engineer F, serving ~2M visitors/month with zero paid marketing A. The company runs entirely on its own stack F. We are not asking investors to believe a one-person billion-dollar company is possible. We are asking them to fund the productization of the one that already exists.

Weakness in the model, disclosed

The model's absolute price floors dominate at small headcount and overstate seed-stage spend F: 15 employees → $301,118 SaaS = $20,075/employee (4.2× the benchmark — not credible); 25 → $13,160; 60 → $7,646; 100 → $6,146. Roughly fifteen of the 42 categories carry minimums that do not scale down, so the model is sound above ~60 employees and unusable below ~40. It is therefore not used to derive the Techstars ACV below — that comes from the per-employee benchmark instead. Disclosed because the seed cohort is exactly what the proforma targets, and a diligence reader would find it.

4 · Product — one binary, one protocol, five projections

One binary

~104 services compose into a single Go host. The lazy-plugin architecture links 316 packages against 3,105, ships 19 MB, holds 14 MB RSS with every app mounted, starts a plugin cold in 123 ms / warm in 4–7 ms. A plugin can panic without taking a route down; a failed build cannot. Each pinned by a test passing under -race, not asserted in a README. F

One protocol

ZAP: length-prefixed name/value header pairs, every name and value a subslice of the frame, decoding in 0 allocations/op — 3.2× the JSON path it replaced. gRPC went 65 packages → 0; the roots were the Gemini SDK (sole importer of cloud.google.com/go/auth, dragging gax-go and all 13 s2a-go packages) and an OTLP exporter whose HTTP variant imported gRPC internally. F

Five projections

One typed handler emits the REST route, the OpenAPI path, the MCP tool, the CLI command and the SDK client. Doc comments lift into the spec at build time, so the reference cannot drift from the code — 959 OpenAPI paths generated from the live registry, CI fails on drift. For an AI-native buyer this is load-bearing: every service is an agent tool the day it ships. F

Case study zero — Hanzo on Hanzo

The company operates fully on its own stack, off GCP, on DigitalOcean F. ATS/BD/TA and business-ops replaced natively; compute cut ~90% and vendor headcount to zero A. The deployment being sold is the deployment that exists. It is also the only case study in this memo, and it is the founder's own — a limitation, stated as one.

5 · Business model

1 · Cloud usage

Metered compute, inference and storage. Margin benefits from the same ~90% compute reduction the company runs on itself A and DO-class rather than hyperscaler pricing F.

2 · Seats

Per-user pricing across team, studio, erp and the back-office surface. Bundled with usage below as a single ARPU per paid account.

3 · Marketplace rev-share

Third-party connectors, models, templates and agents transacted on the platform's billing rails, at a take rate.

4 · Dependency dividends — the kingmaker mechanic

The platform knows from the dependency graph which open-source projects its revenue rests on. A defined share of the marketplace take routes back to those maintainers through the same rails. Not a CSR line — it converts the commons from a cost center into a distribution channel and gives every maintainer a commercial reason to keep Hanzo working. Hattori Hanzō did not win the battle; he got the man who did out of Iga alive.

⚠ Counsel flag — the word "dividends"

"Dependency dividends" describes a commercial revenue-share paid to software maintainers for goods and services. It is not a distribution on equity and not a payment to security holders. In a Reg CF document the term can be read as an implied return to investors, which would be a material problem. Counsel must either approve the term with a definition attached or rename the program. Flagged, not resolved.

Pricing posture E: Hanzo prices at roughly 30–40% of the assembled alternative. The savings are the pitch; capturing more inverts the value proposition. This ratio is what turns the assembled-cost figures above into the ACVs below.

8 · Risks — stated at diligence depth

Key-person risk — dominant and severe

One engineer operates 4,973 repositories and ~104 services F. There is no bus factor. If the founder is unavailable, the ability to ship, support and secure the platform degrades immediately and materially. Partial mitigations: the architecture is deliberately survivable at this headcount — one protocol, one registry with five projections, one plugin model, agentic maintenance against the estate — and that discipline is why the surface exists at all. But an architecture is not a person. Not yet in place and recommended: key-person insurance, a documented successor/escrow arrangement, and a second engineer with commit rights across the core. This risk is not eliminated by this raise.

Auth & self-service are under repair

Driver B — 13% of conservative Y5 revenue and 39% of founder Y5 revenue — does not work unattended today. Until it does, ~2M monthly visitors convert at approximately zero. It is the first line in the use of funds for exactly that reason. Read every self-serve column as contingent on that work landing.

Competition from model labs

Frontier labs ship platform surface steadily with unbounded capital. Hanzo's defensible position is what they will not build: self-hostable, open-source, identity + billing + commerce + compliance + settlement, on any model including theirs. Durable, but a bet that labs stay in the model business — a credible open self-hostable Business OS from a lab weakens the thesis sharply. Related discipline: never position Hanzo as competing with frontier training. It invites an immediate losing comparison and discounts everything said after it.

Private-repo verification pending

2,172 of 4,973 repositories are private F. No third party can verify the private portion, nor the ~104-service composition, without read access or an independent attestation. An investor relying on the scale claim should ask for a scoped audit. The public portion (2,253 org + 548 personal) is verifiable now.

The strongest claims are the least verified

The attested table is the persuasive material and none of it is citable today. If the Anthropic attestation cannot be obtained, or the case studies cannot be published with permission, the story reverts to the measured column — excellent engineering evidence with no commercial proof attached.

No backlog; single channel

Zero signed contracts, zero committed pipeline, no current ARR asserted. Every dollar in the proforma is un-won. All of Driver A rests on one network and one alumni relationship — no contractual arrangement with Techstars exists or is implied, and the ~2,500 active-company figure is itself unsourced CITE.

Reg CF risks, non-exhaustive

Illiquidity — no public market, resale restricted twelve months subject to exceptions. Dilution by future rounds. Possibility of total loss. Reliance on the founder. Per-investor limits based on income and net worth. Ongoing annual reporting obligations. Counsel must draft the definitive risk factors; this is not sufficient for a Form C.

9 · The offer frame — users become owners

Owners above, maintainers below, one billing rail through the middle. A user who becomes a shareholder and a maintainer who becomes a payee are two halves of the same idea: the platform's success should be indistinguishable from the ecosystem's.

Reg CF is not a second-choice financing here, it is the structurally correct one. Hanzo's users are self-hosting developers and founders already running the software for free. A community round makes the people who depend on the platform the people who own it — the same alignment the dependency-dividend mechanic creates one layer down. In the base case the company routes $3.31M to open-source maintainers over five years as a contractual consequence of its own revenue: not a grant program, a billing rail.
