Not just the bill — the build

Everything above prices what you stop paying. It ignores what you would have to build to own this surface yourself — and the market has already priced that build. Public anchors for five categories alone: identity (Auth0 — ~$330M raised, acquired for $6.5B), observability (Datadog — over $1B/yr in R&D), billing & metering (a dedicated team at every SaaS company ever built), flags & experimentation (LaunchDarkly — ~$200M raised), workflow automation (Zapier — 800+ people). Hanzo hands you the open-source equivalent of that entire build as one binary — adoption cost $0, replacement cost expected in engineer-centuries. A rigorous COCOMO-based replacement-cost report on the full 4,973-repository estate is in progress and will be linked here; until it lands, that expectation is labeled what it is: an expectation.

Still not counted — the honest remainder

Only three things stay outside the model: card interchange & network fees (nobody erases those — the table counts only the PSP markup on top of them), frontier-model spend that stays frontier (the LLM row above counts the workloads Zen serves in-house at ~90% lower per-token cost, founder-attested; what must remain on third-party frontier models is a pass-through), and deep vertical software (clinical, CAD, EDA). Every other category is in the table, graded full per the CTO's attestation — the single ×0.5 that remains is the one defect this session measured (vector †), not doubt.

Method & sources.
  • Portfolio-size context, widely reported: Okta Businesses at Work — ~93 apps average, 211 average for companies with 2,000+ employees; Productiv State of SaaS — 254 average; Zylo SaaS Management Index — ~270 average (enterprise-skewed base), ~$4,800 SaaS spend per employee per year, with ~44% of licenses unused (Gartner has put waste at ~25%). Figures are from the published reports as widely cited; verify against the current editions before external use — web verification tooling was unavailable when this page was generated.
  • Category prices are public list prices at typical tiers, parameterized on headcount; they deliberately sit at the conservative end (real Datadog and Segment bills routinely run multiples of the figure used here).
  • Infra delta assumes DO-class pricing vs hyperscaler list on compute and egress (~$0.01/GB vs ~$0.09/GB); modeled as 60% of the current bill. Hanzo itself runs on DigitalOcean — the deployment being priced is the one that exists.
  • Glue engineering: FTEs maintaining vendor integrations, webhooks, auth bridges and billing reconciliation, at $220k fully loaded. The default (5% of engineers, min 1) is conservative for a 30+ vendor stack. The same rate prices the ops cost of running Hanzo, subtracted on the other side.
  • Second-order effects not priced: SSO price premiums (vendors charge 2–4× for SSO; Hanzo ships one IAM), per-vendor security reviews and DPAs, procurement cycles, and license waste recovery — all real, all additive, all omitted to keep the model conservative.
